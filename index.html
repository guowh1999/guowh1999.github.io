Research Directions

Four directions I keep returning to.

Different methods, one question: what is actually true in the data, and what would change if it were.

Proteomics

Proteomics asks what proteins are actually present, in what quantity, and how that changes between health and disease. Mass spectrometry answers at scale, but the measurements are noisy and incomplete — so much of the real work is statistical: deciding what a missing value means.

Spatial and Single-Cell Transcriptomics

Bulk sequencing averages thousands of cells into a single number. Single-cell methods pull them apart; spatial methods put them back where they belong. Together they let us ask not only what a tissue contains, but how its neighbourhoods are organised.

Natural Product Targets and Mechanism

Most drugs still trace back to molecules that nature made first. Natural products are structurally dense and often engage more than one protein, so the open questions are which target is actually responsible, and what the downstream mechanism really is.

Epidemiology and Health Economics

Epidemiology measures how much disease exists and who carries it. Health economics asks whether acting on it is worth the cost. Both are exercises in making assumptions explicit enough that a decision can survive them.
